# Jute Crop Facts
Jute is a warm-season fiber crop that performs best in humid tropical to subtropical environments. Optimal growth is generally observed when daytime temperatures remain roughly in the 24-35 C range and humidity remains moderate to high through vegetative stages. Early-season cold stress or prolonged dry spells can reduce stand establishment and fiber quality.

Jute typically responds to balanced nutrient management. Nitrogen supports vegetative growth, but excessive nitrogen without balancing phosphorus and potassium may increase lodging or produce imbalanced canopy growth. Potassium is often important for stem strength and stress tolerance. Final fertilization plans should be based on measured soil test values rather than generic schedules.

A moderately acidic to near-neutral soil pH often supports nutrient availability; however, local texture, drainage, and flood behavior are equally important. Jute can tolerate short wet periods in some systems, but repeated waterlogging with poor drainage can increase disease pressure and lower fiber quality.

Practical management checklist:
- Confirm sowing window with local seasonal rainfall onset and field trafficability.
- Validate N/P/K with laboratory test before fertilizer decisions.
- Monitor weed pressure in early growth stages; delayed control can severely reduce yield.
- Plan harvest timing based on intended fiber quality and retting logistics.

Interpretation note for decision support systems: if raw model confidence is high but season suitability is low, treat recommendation as tentative and escalate to agronomist review.
