# Pulse Crops Facts
Pulse crops (e.g., blackgram, mungbean, lentil, chickpea in relevant systems) can improve diversification and nitrogen cycling, but are still sensitive to waterlogging, disease pressure, and nutrient imbalance.

Although pulses can reduce external nitrogen dependency through biological fixation when inoculation and soil conditions are suitable, phosphorus and potassium remain important for root growth and reproductive performance. Sulfur and micronutrients may also become limiting in some soils.

Pulses often benefit from well-drained fields and careful moisture management around flowering and pod set. Extended humidity can elevate fungal risk in susceptible cultivars.

Practical recommendations:
- Use field-specific soil tests and local inoculation guidance.
- Match pulse type and duration to seasonal moisture profile.
- Monitor disease risk under high humidity and constrained airflow.
- Treat low-confidence AI output as advisory; verify with local agronomy context.
