# Maize Crop Facts
Maize is a high-response cereal crop with strong dependence on nutrient balance, radiation, and stable moisture during vegetative and reproductive stages. It typically performs best under warm conditions with well-drained soils and minimal prolonged waterlogging.

Nitrogen is often the dominant yield driver, but phosphorus and potassium remain critical for root development, reproductive success, and stress tolerance. Nutrient timing and placement matter: heavy early losses or late correction can reduce attainable yield.

Soil pH near slightly acidic to neutral generally supports nutrient availability, though micronutrient issues can still arise depending on soil type. Field compaction, poor drainage, or irregular irrigation often reduce performance even when weather appears favorable.

Risk-aware practices:
- Confirm N/P/K through soil testing before planting.
- Validate seasonal fit and expected moisture profile for the crop cycle.
- Monitor heat stress risk at flowering and grain-fill stages.
- Prefer pilot-scale adoption if model-adjusted confidence is below threshold.

In an AI-assisted pipeline, high raw probability should not override seasonal unsuitability or poor data quality signals.
