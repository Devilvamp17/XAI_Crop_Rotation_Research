# Cotton Crop Facts
Cotton is a long-duration warm-season crop that benefits from stable thermal conditions and moderate water availability. Excess humidity and frequent rainfall around boll development can elevate pest and disease risk and negatively affect lint quality.

Potassium is commonly important for boll retention and fiber development, while balanced nitrogen management supports canopy development without excessive vegetative growth. Nutrient imbalance can amplify pest susceptibility and reduce harvest efficiency.

Well-structured soils with reliable drainage are usually preferred. Water stress at squaring and flowering can strongly impact yield potential. Region-specific planting windows and cultivar choices are essential due to large differences in temperature and rainfall patterns.

Field guidance:
- Match sowing date and cultivar duration to local season length.
- Use measured soil test values for N/P/K and pH adjustment plans.
- Monitor insect pressure and humidity-driven disease risk proactively.
- Use conservative recommendations when model disagreement or low adjusted confidence is present.

For explanation workflows, separate model evidence (raw confidence) from operational constraints (season and weather suitability).
