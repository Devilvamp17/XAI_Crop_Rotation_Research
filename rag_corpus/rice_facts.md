# Rice Crop Facts
Rice is highly sensitive to water regime, planting method, and thermal conditions over the crop cycle. Most production systems perform best in warm conditions with reliable moisture availability, especially during establishment and reproductive phases. Heat stress during flowering can reduce grain set, while prolonged cloudy wet periods may increase disease incidence.

Nitrogen strongly influences tillering and yield, but split application strategies are commonly used to reduce losses and synchronize uptake. Phosphorus supports early root vigor, while potassium improves stress resilience and grain filling. Blanket recommendations are often suboptimal; soil-test-guided nutrient balancing is preferred.

Rice can be grown across a broad pH range, but extreme acidity or alkalinity may constrain nutrient uptake and require amendment. Field leveling, drainage management, and irrigation control can substantially alter outcome even under similar weather conditions.

Operational recommendations:
- Align sowing/transplanting with local monsoon or irrigation schedule.
- Validate soil fertility and pH status before committing nutrient program.
- Track pest and disease pressure under high humidity conditions.
- Use conservative planning when forecast uncertainty is high or confidence is low.

For explainability in farmer communication, prioritize actionable factors (water reliability, nutrient balance, sowing window) rather than overemphasizing model confidence alone.
