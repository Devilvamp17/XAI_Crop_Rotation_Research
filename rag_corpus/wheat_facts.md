# Wheat Crop Facts
Wheat is generally favored in cooler season windows and can be sensitive to terminal heat during grain filling. Crop duration, sowing date, and temperature trajectory are critical determinants of final yield and quality.

Nitrogen timing influences tiller formation and grain protein, while phosphorus and potassium support root activity and stress resilience. In many systems, delayed sowing increases exposure to late-season heat and can compress grain filling.

Soil pH and micronutrient status may affect nutrient efficiency and stand performance. Uniform seedbed preparation and irrigation scheduling during crown root initiation and grain development are often high-impact interventions.

Decision support recommendations:
- Validate sowing window against local seasonal calendar.
- Confirm nutrient strategy from measured soil data.
- Track heat-risk projections for reproductive and grain-fill periods.
- Escalate to manual review if model outputs are low-confidence after seasonal adjustment.
